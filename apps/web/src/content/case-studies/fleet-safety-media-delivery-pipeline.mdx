<CaseStudySection
id="executive-summary"
eyebrow="Overview"
title="Executive Summary"

>

<p>
  The Fleet Safety Media Delivery Pipeline is a serverless integration workflow that receives
  safety-event webhooks, normalizes event data, selects available media, and delivers operational
  notifications to Telegram.
</p>

<p>
  The workflow replaced a more limited manual process with an automated delivery path capable of
  processing approximately 1,500–2,000 videos per week, compared with approximately 150 videos per
  week before the automation.
</p>

  <p>
    I implemented webhook parsing, payload normalization, event
    mapping, driver and vehicle extraction, message formatting,
    camera and media selection, video and photo delivery, timeout
    handling, fan-out, and aggregate analytics.
  </p>
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection
id="problem"
eyebrow="Business problem"
title="Safety Media Was Not Reaching Teams at the Required Volume"

>

<p>
  Fleet safety events could contain useful video, image, vehicle, driver, location, time, and speed
  information, but manually reviewing and distributing those events limited how many could reach
  operational teams.
</p>

<p>
  The source system could produce different webhook payload shapes, and the required media was not
  always available in the same format or camera position.
</p>

  <p>
    A reliable workflow needed to receive the webhook, normalize
    the data, determine what media was available, format a useful
    notification, and deliver the result without allowing failures
    in one event to stop the rest of the processing flow.
  </p>
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection
id="business-context"
eyebrow="Users and context"
title="An Operational Safety Notification Workflow"

>

<p>
  The pipeline supports fleet, dispatch, and safety-related operational teams that need timely
  visibility into driving events and their associated media.
</p>

<p>
  Each event may contain a different combination of driver, vehicle, time, location, speed, event
  classification, video, image, and camera information.
</p>

  <p>

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection

eyebrow="Boundaries"

>

</p>

<p>Important constraints included:</p>

  <ul>
    <li>

      media types.
    </li>
    <li>
      Optional or missing driver, vehicle, location, and media
      fields.
    </li>
    <li>Video URLs that might be unavailable or expire.</li>
    <li>Multiple available camera positions.</li>
    <li>Telegram request size, media, and timeout restrictions.</li>
    <li>Fan-out to more than one configured destination.</li>
    <li>Sensitive driver, vehicle, location, and event metadata.</li>
    <li>
      The need to continue processing when one destination or
      media item fails.
    </li>
    <li>
      Serverless execution-time and external-network constraints.
    </li>
    <li>
      The absence of confirmed webhook signature verification in
      the supplied implementation.

  context="Video provided the strongest operational context, but it was not available or deliverable for every event."

    "Fallback logic adds additional network requests and error paths.",
    "A photo does not provide the same operational context as a video.",

<CaseStudySection
id="challenges"
eyebrow="Trade-offs"
title="Challenges"

>

<p>
  <strong>Variable webhook payloads.</strong> Similar event data could appear under different nested
  fields. The workflow needed defensive parsing and normalization.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Unavailable media.</strong> Some events did not include a usable video, or the media
  request could fail after the webhook had already been received.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Camera selection.</strong> Multiple media candidates required a clear priority order
  rather than selecting the first available item.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Telegram timeouts.</strong> Media delivery introduced longer-running external requests and
  destination-specific failures.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Duplicate events.</strong> The supplied implementation did not provide confirmed explicit
  idempotency protection, which creates a risk of duplicate delivery when a webhook is retried.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Webhook authenticity.</strong> The supplied handler did not confirm signature or
  shared-secret verification, so the public rewrite must add authentication before processing.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Sensitive logs.</strong> Driver names, vehicle details, locations, coordinates, and media
  URLs must not be recorded in unrestricted logs.
</p>

  <p>
    <strong>Scheduled analytics.</strong> Daily aggregation logic
    existed in the supplied material, but the corresponding
    scheduled handler was not verified.
  </p>
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection
id="lessons"
eyebrow="Reflection"
title="Lessons Learned"

>

  <ul>
    <li>
      Webhook input should be normalized before business and
      delivery logic.
    </li>
    <li>Optional external data requires explicit fallback rules.</li>
    <li>
      Media delivery needs independent timeout and failure
      handling.
    </li>
    <li>Fan-out operations should record results per destination.</li>
    <li>
      Idempotency should be designed before webhook retries occur
      in production.
    </li>
    <li>Webhook authentication is a required security boundary.</li>
    <li>
      Logs should contain diagnostic categories rather than raw
      driver and location data.
    </li>
    <li>
      Operational screenshots are evidence of use, but they do not
      replace automated tests or delivery analytics.
    </li>
  </ul>
</CaseStudySection>
