<CaseStudySection
  id="executive-summary"
  eyebrow="Overview"
  title="Executive Summary"
>
  <p>
    The Internal HR Operations Platform is a full-stack business
    application built to centralize employee-lifecycle workflows
    that were previously distributed across separate operational
    processes.
  </p>

<p>
  The system covers recruitment, onboarding, employee records, leave, attendance, documents, access
  operations, audit, reporting, and offboarding. Its current backend contains more than 200 REST API
  operations across 12 functional modules.
</p>

  <p>
    I developed the backend and frontend, translated operational
    requirements into application workflows, designed the
    relational data model, implemented authorization and
    migrations, prepared release smoke tests, and deployed the
    system for internal company use.
  </p>
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection
  id="problem"
  eyebrow="Business problem"
  title="Fragmented Employee-Lifecycle Operations"
>
  <p>
    HR and operational activities involved multiple connected
    processes, but those processes did not share one consistent
    system of record.
  </p>

<p>
  The main problem was not simply missing CRUD screens. The platform needed to represent changing
  employee states, permission boundaries, documents, approvals, history, and handoffs between
  operational users.
</p>

  <p>
    Without a centralized application, information could become
    inconsistent, workflow status was harder to verify, and
    reporting depended on manually combining data from different
    sources.
  </p>
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection
  id="business-context"
  eyebrow="Users and context"
  title="An Internal Operational Application"
>
  <p>
    The platform was designed for internal HR and operational
    users. Exact user counts and internal organizational
    structures are not included in the public case study.
  </p>

<p>
  The application supports workflows that continue over time rather than one-time data entry. A
  recruitment record may become an employee record, an employee may enter onboarding, request leave,
  receive documents or access, and later move through an offboarding process.
</p>

  <p>
    This made consistency between modules and lifecycle states a
    central design concern.
  </p>
</CaseStudySection>

    <TechnicalDecision

      reasoning="Centralized dependencies keep authorization behavior visible and reduce repeated checks inside endpoint handlers."

<CaseStudySection
  id="challenges"
  eyebrow="Trade-offs"
  title="Challenges"
>
  <p>
    <strong>Scope growth.</strong> The platform expanded from
    individual HR needs into multiple connected lifecycle modules.
    Maintaining shared patterns became more important as the
    number of routes and entities increased.
  </p>

<p>
  <strong>Authorization consistency.</strong> Permission rules needed to remain consistent across
  related modules without embedding role assumptions directly inside business logic.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Lifecycle transitions.</strong> Employee, recruitment, onboarding, leave, and offboarding
  records could not be treated as independent CRUD resources. Invalid transitions needed to be
  prevented.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Schema evolution.</strong> New requirements affected existing relations and constraints,
  requiring controlled migrations rather than manual database edits.
</p>

  <p>
    <strong>Frontend and backend integration.</strong> Changes to
    API contracts needed to remain synchronized with forms,
    tables, filters, and application states.
  </p>
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection
  id="lessons"
  eyebrow="Reflection"
  title="Lessons Learned"
>
  <p>
    A business application becomes difficult to maintain when its
    workflow states and authorization rules are treated as
    secondary details.
  </p>

<p>The most important lessons from this project were:</p>

  <ul>
    <li>
      Define module boundaries before the route count grows.
    </li>

    <li>Keep permissions explicit and reusable.</li>

    <li>Treat database migrations as application code.</li>

    <li>
      Validate complete workflows, not only individual endpoints.
    </li>

    <li>
      Document operational constraints while requirements are
      still changing.
    </li>

    <li>
      Separate canonical project facts from the narrative used to
      explain them.
    </li>

  </ul>
</CaseStudySection>
